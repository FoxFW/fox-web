Chapter 1

About the Companion App

The FoxFW companion is an Android app that pairs with a Flipper Zero running FoxFW. The Flipper stays in charge of the radio hardware; the phone adds a bigger screen, a faster processor, and an internet connection for the jobs that are awkward on the device itself.

Most of what the app does falls into a few areas:

  • Sub-GHz decoding. Capture a key fob through the Flipper's CC1101, decode it on the phone, and where the protocol allows it, generate and send the next valid rolling code back through the Flipper.
  • Device control. Mirror the Flipper's screen, drive it remotely, browse and manage the SD card, and install firmware updates.
  • Tags. Read, write and emulate NFC and 125 kHz cards through the Flipper, and drive a Chameleon device over Bluetooth.
  • Fox ESP32. When a Fox ESP32 board is wired to the Flipper (or plugged into the phone over USB-OTG), the app gives it a single shared connection and one console for Wi-Fi and Bluetooth work.
The app is a companion, not a replacement. It talks to the Flipper over a simple link and asks it to do the radio work; nothing here reflashes or bypasses the firmware already on your device.
Chapter 2

Installing the App

The app is distributed as an Android package (.apk) on the project's Releases page. It is not on the Play Store, so you install it by sideloading.

Requirements

  • An Android phone on Android 8.0 (Oreo) or newer.
  • A Flipper Zero running FoxFW.
  • For a wired link: a USB-OTG cable or adapter. For a wireless link: Bluetooth.

Sideloading the APK

  1. Download FoxFW Android App.apk from the Releases page.
  2. Open it from your notifications or a file manager.
  3. Android will ask you to allow installs from this source the first time. Approve it, then continue the install.
  4. Open the app once it finishes.
If an install is refused with a signature error, an older build signed with a different key is already installed. Uninstall the old copy first, then install the new one.

Permissions

The app asks for permissions only as a feature needs them:

  • Bluetooth (and, on some Android versions, Nearby devices / Location) to find and connect to the Flipper or a Chameleon wirelessly.
  • USB device access when you plug a Flipper or an ESP32 into the phone; Android prompts per device.
  • Notifications so the app can tell you when a long job, such as a captured handshake, finishes.

Debug vs signed builds

Two kinds of build exist. A signed release build is the normal download. A debug build is what you get if you compile the project yourself without setting up a signing key; it is larger, is marked debuggable, and includes a few developer-only panels. For day-to-day use, prefer a signed release.

Chapter 3

Pairing with Your Flipper

The app works over Bluetooth or USB. Pick whichever suits the moment — the same features are available either way, and USB is the faster link for large transfers.

Over Bluetooth

  1. On the Flipper, make sure Bluetooth is on and the device is pairable.
  2. In the app, open the Device tab and choose to connect over Bluetooth.
  3. Pick your Flipper from the list and confirm the pairing code if asked.
Bluetooth is convenient and cable-free. For streaming the screen or moving large files it is slower than USB, so switch to a cable when you want speed.

Over USB

  1. Connect the Flipper to the phone with a USB-OTG cable or adapter.
  2. Android asks for permission to use the USB device — allow it.
  3. The app connects automatically.

The helper app on the Flipper

Some jobs — live Sub-GHz capture and transmit, NFC and LF tag work, and settings sync — run through a small helper app that ships with FoxFW. The companion launches it on the Flipper when needed; you do not normally start it by hand. If a feature reports that the Flipper is busy, return the Flipper to its desktop and try again.

Chapter 4

Device Tab

The Device tab is home base: it shows what is connected and gives you the tools that act on the Flipper itself.

Device info & receiver

See the connected Flipper's details at a glance, along with the state of the Sub-GHz receiver — including whether an external CC1101 module is attached, so you know which radio a capture or transmit will use.

Screen mirror & remote

Mirror the Flipper's screen on your phone and drive it remotely — the directional pad, OK and Back — so you can operate the device from the larger display. A software keyboard lets you type into the Flipper where it would otherwise mean clicking letters one at a time.

File manager

Browse the Flipper's SD card, open and preview files, and move captures and other files between the phone and the device.

Firmware updates

Check for and install FoxFW updates over the existing link, without a cable to a computer. The app downloads the update and installs it to the Flipper for you.

An interrupted firmware update can leave the Flipper needing a recovery. Keep the battery charged and the link stable while an update runs.
Chapter 5

Decode Tab

The Decode tab is where you capture a key fob and work out what it is. The Flipper does the listening on its CC1101; the phone does the decoding.

Capturing a fob

  1. Pick the frequency and modulation (433.92 MHz AM650 covers a great many fobs).
  2. Choose which radio to use — the Flipper's internal CC1101, an external module, or Auto.
  3. Press Capture Live and press the fob you want to read.

You can also open a saved .sub file from the phone or straight from the Flipper's SD card instead of capturing live.

The capture window

The Capture Live button has a small duration picker on its right (for example 4s). Tap it to choose how long the capture runs, from 1 to 30 seconds; the choice is remembered for next time. While a capture is running a progress bar fills across the button, and a Stop button lets you end it early and keep what was recorded. Stop disappears in the final second so a late tap cannot land after the window has already closed.

Decoding, replay & rolling codes

Once a fob is captured, the app decodes it on the phone and lists what it found. Open a result to work it:

  • Fixed-code fobs and gates simply retransmit the captured signal.
  • Rolling-code fobs that the app can decrypt on the phone can generate and send the next valid code, so a replay is not just the same packet again.
  • Anything the app cannot forge falls back to a clearly labelled replay of the capture.
The counter used for a rolling code is shown and can be nudged up or down, so you can re-sync if the receiver has rolled ahead of the last code it saw.

Sub-GHz viewer & trimmer

Open any capture in the viewer to see its waveform. The trimmer lets you select a section and cut everything outside it, so you can save a clean, tightly-trimmed .sub of just the part you want.

Chapter 6

Archive

The Archive keeps a history of what you have captured and decoded, so you can come back to a fob later, re-open it, re-send it, or save it to the Flipper. Undecoded captures are kept too, so a signal that did not resolve the first time is not lost.

Chapter 7

Tools

The Tools tab collects the rest of the app's features.

ESP32 Wi-Fi & Bluetooth

With a Fox ESP32 board wired to the Flipper (or plugged into the phone over USB-OTG), the app drives it for Wi-Fi and Bluetooth work. It keeps a single shared connection to the board and one unified console across all of the ESP32 pages, so you connect once and stay connected as you move around the app. Scan results, captured handshakes and status all land in the same place no matter which page produced them.

Wi-Fi and Bluetooth testing features are for networks and devices you own or are explicitly authorised to test. See Safety & Legal.

Chameleon

Drive a Chameleon device over Bluetooth to read, write and emulate NFC cards, and manage the slots on the device.

Tag Maker

Read, write and emulate NFC (13.56 MHz) and low-frequency (125 kHz) tags through the Flipper, and build tags from scratch.

Sub-GHz utilities

Beyond the Decode tab there are supporting Sub-GHz tools — a band and spectrum view for finding where a signal sits, and helpers for specific vehicle and remote families. These lean on the same Flipper radio the Decode tab uses.

Other tools

  • Wallpaper painter — draw a wallpaper for the Flipper on your phone and send it across.
  • Bluetooth LE inspector — browse the services and characteristics of a nearby BLE device.
  • Wi-Fi handshake helper — take a captured handshake from the ESP32 side and work it on the phone.
Chapter 8

Settings Sync

The app can read and write the Flipper's own settings over the same link — display and sound, locale, theme, and more — so you can adjust the device from the phone's keyboard and screen instead of clicking through menus. Changes are sent to the Flipper and applied there.

Chapter 9

Safety & Legal

This app, like the Flipper and the Fox ESP32, is a tool for learning, testing and working with hardware you own or are allowed to work on.

  • Only capture, replay or transmit signals for devices you own or have explicit permission to test. Replaying a rolling code you do not own can be illegal.
  • Only test Wi-Fi networks and Bluetooth devices you own or are authorised to assess.
  • Radio transmission is regulated. You are responsible for staying within the rules for your region and the band you are using.
You are responsible for how you use this software. Use it lawfully and ethically.
Chapter 10

Troubleshooting

SymptomWhat to check
App will not installAllow installs from this source, and uninstall any older build first if you hit a signature error.
Flipper will not connect over USBUse a USB-OTG cable or adapter, and approve the USB permission prompt. Try a different cable — some are charge-only.
Bluetooth will not find the FlipperTurn Bluetooth on at both ends, grant the Nearby devices / Location permission, and make sure the Flipper is pairable.
Capture or transmit reports the Flipper is busyReturn the Flipper to its desktop so the helper app can launch, then retry.
A fob captured but did not decodeCapture again with the fob closer, confirm the frequency and modulation, and save the raw capture so it is not lost.
ESP32 features do nothingCheck the board is wired to the Flipper (or plugged into the phone) and that it is running the Fox ESP32 firmware.
